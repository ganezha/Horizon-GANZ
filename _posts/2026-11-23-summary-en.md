### [OpenAI’s “Perplexity and Intelligence” Spurs Debate on Model Evaluation](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Hacker News discussion of OpenAI’s “Perplexity and Intelligence” examines whether better next-token prediction tracks broader language model capabilities. Lower perplexity means a model assigns higher probabilities to the tokens that actually appear in an evaluation text. The supplied material does not include OpenAI’s methods or results, so it is unclear how strong the reported relationship is or whether it holds across models and tasks.

hackernews · acossta · Nov 22, 17:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50601525)

**「What perplexity measures」** Perplexity measures how much probability a language model assigns to the actual next tokens in a test set: lower is better, but the score does not directly measure broader capabilities. OpenAI says its primary held-out evaluation datasets have come from its internal codebase since GPT-3.5 and that the article reports scores on a fixed snapshot.

**「Community Discussion」** One commenter was surprised that next-token prediction appeared to remain a useful benchmark despite the growing use of reinforcement learning. Another quoted Microsoft’s Mustafa Suleyman as reporting a log-perplexity of 0.819 for MAI-2 Base on OpenAI’s held-out evaluation set, which he described as comparable to GPT-5.6 Sol’s base model on that metric.

### [O’Reilly Essay Asks What Silicon Valley’s Markets Should Reward](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-happened-to-silicon-valley/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An O’Reilly Radar essay argues that Silicon Valley’s market incentives should be redesigned to prioritize human flourishing rather than corporate profit. It frames mechanism design as “reverse game theory”: instead of asking how companies behave under existing rules, start with the outcome society wants and ask what rules would encourage it.

hackernews · greenyouse · Nov 22, 19:20 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50602947)

**「Mechanism design」** Mechanism design is often called reverse game theory: instead of taking the rules as given and asking how people will behave, it starts with a desired outcome and asks what rules would give people incentives to produce it.

**「Community discussion」** Several Hacker News commenters endorsed rethinking what markets reward, while another offered a more immediate explanation for Silicon Valley’s trajectory: the era of near-zero interest rates, they argued, helped tech giants grow and encouraged startups to pursue untapped markets rather than compete in established ones.
